From plasma and molecular dynamics to active matter and gravity, I use computational tools to investigate how physical systems evolve and interact.

Plasma Physics · Summer 2025

Boron–Deuterium Diffusion

At Princeton Plasma Physics Laboratory (PPPL), I studied deuterium diffusion in alpha-rhombohedral boron using molecular dynamics simulations. The work focused on understanding how retained deuterium moves through the boron structure.

Methods and tools

LAMMPS, molecular dynamics, interatomic potentials, Python, computational analysis.

Active Matter · 2026

Suspended Animation: Survival of Active Patterns Without Energy

This project investigates whether active biological patterns can persist when their energy source, ATP, is removed. Using computational simulations, I study how ATP-dependent motility, phase separation, and glass-like dynamics affect the stability and evolution of spatial patterns.

Methods and tools

Python, NumPy, Numba, numerical PDE solvers, active matter models, phase diagrams, and computational physics.

Computational Physics

Three-Body Problem

Numerical investigation of gravitational interactions in three-body systems, including the Pythagorean/Burrau initial configuration. I used numerical integration to follow the system's trajectories and examine its complex dynamical behavior.

Methods and tools

Python, SciPy, DOP853, numerical integration, data visualization.

Quantum Information

Quantum Phase Estimation Under Imperfect Gates

Simulation project studying quantum phase estimation when quantum gates are imperfect. The work compares the standard quantum Fourier transform with an approximate quantum Fourier transform and examines their behavior under gate errors.

Methods and tools

Quantum circuit simulation, Python, numerical analysis.
