Plasma Physics · Summer 2025

Boron–Deuterium Diffusion

During my internship at Princeton Plasma Physics Laboratory (PPPL), I used molecular dynamics simulations to study how deuterium moves through alpha-rhombohedral boron, a material relevant to plasma-facing components.

Research overview

The project focused on the motion and retention of deuterium in the boron structure. I built and analyzed simulations to examine where deuterium atoms remain confined and how they move between nearby regions of the material at different temperatures.

Methods and tools

LAMMPS, molecular dynamics, ReaxFF and other interatomic potentials, Python, numerical analysis, and high-performance computing.

Active Matter · 2026

Suspended Animation: Survival of Active Patterns Without Energy

This project asks whether spatial patterns formed by active matter can persist after the energy source that supports motion is reduced or removed. The model explores how ATP-dependent activity changes pattern formation, relaxation, and long-term stability.

Research overview

I developed numerical simulations of an active-matter model with an energy-dependent activity term. The system is first allowed to form patterns under active conditions, then the ATP level is reduced to study how the patterns respond as the system becomes less active.

Methods and tools

Python, NumPy, Numba, finite-difference methods, numerical PDE solvers, active matter, phase diagrams, and glass-like dynamics.

Computational Physics · Fall 2023

Three-Body Problem

This project explores the gravitational motion of three interacting bodies, where the coupled dynamics can produce complex and highly sensitive trajectories.

Research overview

I numerically investigated the Pythagorean, also known as Burrau's, three-body initial configuration. By integrating the equations of motion, I followed the positions of the bodies over time and visualized their trajectories to examine the system's dynamical behavior.

Methods and tools

Python, NumPy, SciPy, DOP853, numerical integration, and Matplotlib.

Quantum Information

Quantum Phase Estimation Under Imperfect Gates

This project studies how imperfect quantum gates and noise affect quantum phase estimation (QPE), with a focus on comparing the standard quantum Fourier transform (QFT) with an approximate QFT (AQFT).

Research overview

I used circuit simulations to examine how the success probability changes as the number of phase qubits and the noise level vary. The heatmap summarizes the dependence of QPE performance on these parameters and helps show where phase estimation remains reliable under imperfect gates.

Methods and tools

Python, quantum circuit simulation, QFT, approximate QFT, noise modeling, parameter sweeps, and data visualization.
